### Google requirements / official guidance

- **`llms.txt` has zero effect on Google Search visibility or rankings.** Google now states explicitly that Google Search does **not use** `llms.txt`, AI text files, special markdown, or special AI-specific markup. Maintaining one for other systems is fine, but for Google it neither helps nor hurts. This is stronger than treating `llms.txt` as merely “not required.” [คำแนะนำของ Google ในการเพิ่มประสิทธิภาพสำหรับฟีเจอร์ Generative AI ใน Google Search | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?authuser=2&hl=th)
- **Do not “chunk” pages specifically for AI retrieval.** Google explicitly says content does not need to be broken into small sections for generative systems to understand it. Page length should follow user/content needs rather than an artificial AEO chunk-size rule. [คำแนะนำของ Google ในการเพิ่มประสิทธิภาพสำหรับฟีเจอร์ Generative AI ใน Google Search | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?authuser=2&hl=th)
- **Domain migrations: submit Change of Address for every verified old-domain variant.** For a genuine domain migration, Google now recommends handling variants such as `www`, non-`www`, and relevant subdomains individually—even variants you are not actively using. This should become a mandatory migration QA item. Google also reiterates that permanent redirects do not lose PageRank and should normally stay live for **at least one year**. [Site Moves and Migrations | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Preferred Sources is now an actionable publisher-distribution lever.** A domain or subdomain—not a subfolder—can be selected by users as a preferred source. Google may then highlight it in Top Stories and, where available, AI Mode/AI Overviews. Google now provides an embeddable “Add to Preferred Sources” mechanism. This is not a ranking-factor claim, but it is a legitimate audience-retention / AI-discovery tactic for eligible publishing sites. [Guide to Preferred Sources in Google Search for Web Publishers | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Site reputation abuse enforcement now differs inside vs. outside the EEA.** Starting **August 30, 2026**, a manual action can directly suppress the affected section outside the EEA, while inside the EEA Google says that direct manual-action effect will not apply; instead, the section may be algorithmically separated so it ranks independently from the host site's reputation over time. This is particularly important for parasite SEO / third-party subdirectory strategies. [Update to the Site Reputation Policy  |  Google Search Central Blog  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Vendor research / observations — not Google rules

Ahrefs' December 2025 GSC-based study found that an AI Overview correlated with **58% lower CTR for the #1 organic result**, with measurable reductions throughout positions 1–10. Treat this as a forecasting/input metric rather than a universal CTR constant: AIO presence should now be considered when estimating traffic potential from rankings. [Update: AI Overviews Reduce Clicks by 58%](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is also useful segmentation evidence. Ahrefs' 146M-SERP analysis found AIO occurrence rose sharply with query complexity: **57.9% of question queries** and **46.4% of 7+ word queries** triggered AIOs in its September 2025 dataset, versus only **7.9% of local searches**. This supports splitting keyword opportunity models by **AIO exposure risk**, rather than applying one organic CTR curve across an entire keyword set. [What Triggers AI Overviews? 86 Factors and 146 Million SERPs Analyzed](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Semrush adds an important newer qualification: AIOs are no longer primarily an informational-search problem. Across 600K+ US desktop keywords, commercial-intent SERPs triggering AIOs increased **71%** between November 2025 and April 2026, while transactional AIO occurrence slightly decreased. Commercial SEO forecasts should therefore include AI-SERP exposure too. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
