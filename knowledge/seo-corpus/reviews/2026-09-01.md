## Google requirements / official guidance

- **Crawl-budget work should be triaged, not applied by default.** Google now frames crawl-budget optimization as an advanced concern mainly for sites around **1M+ URLs changing weekly**, **10K+ URLs changing daily**, or sites with a large share of `Discovered – currently not indexed`. Its crawling infrastructure treats each hostname separately, and crawl capacity is shared across Google crawlers hitting that host. Crucially, blocking URLs does **not** automatically “transfer” crawl budget elsewhere unless Google was already hitting the host’s crawl-capacity limit. `noindex` is also the wrong tool for saving crawl resources because Google still has to fetch the page to see it; permanent removals should return `404/410`, while `304 Not Modified` support can materially reduce recrawl cost. [Crawl Budget Management | Google Crawling Infrastructure  |  Crawling infrastructure  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget)

- **Canonical fixes can legitimately take up to two weeks to break a duplicate cluster.** Google’s updated canonical troubleshooting guide says pages may remain clustered for **up to two weeks after content/canonical issues are fixed**. Pages tend to separate faster when the content difference is clear and significant. `Request Indexing` can accelerate re-evaluation, but Google explicitly recommends reserving it for important URLs because of quotas. This gives us a much better QA rule: do not call a canonical fix “failed” after two or three days. [Fix Canonicalization Issues | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)

- **JavaScript canonical and `noindex` sequencing now belongs in technical audit QA.** Google recommends that a JS-injected canonical either match the canonical already present in the original HTML, or—if the canonical cannot be set correctly server-side—be omitted from the source HTML and added by JS. Changing canonical A → B during rendering creates ambiguity. More importantly, an original-HTML `noindex` may cause Google to skip rendering, so attempting to remove that `noindex` later with JavaScript may never work. [Understand JavaScript SEO Basics | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **Social/video assets are now first-class measurable Google Search assets.** Search Console platform properties can track verified **TikTok, Instagram, X and YouTube** accounts across Google Search, Discover and Google News. Google exposes queries, countries, devices, individual posts/videos, 24-hour performance and query-group trends. A durable workflow is therefore to treat owned social content as part of the searchable content inventory: identify rising query groups, compare formats such as Reels vs posts or Shorts vs long-form, annotate title/caption changes, and repurpose content when Google demand emerges. [Analyze Social and Video Content in Search Console | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/analyze-social-video-content)

## Vendor studies / observations — not Google ranking rules

Semrush’s **89K cited LinkedIn URL** study adds a useful “search everywhere” observation. Across ChatGPT Search, Google AI Mode and Perplexity, roughly **95% of cited LinkedIn posts were original rather than reshares**, educational/advice content dominated citations, and cited posts generally had only modest engagement rather than viral numbers. Around three-quarters of cited post authors had published 5+ times in the previous four weeks. The practical hypothesis is: build repeatable SME publishing programs with original, explicit, educational content rather than optimizing social output only for likes and reach. This is correlation/observational evidence, not proof that posting frequency or a particular content length causes citation. [We Analyzed 89K LinkedIn URLs Cited in AI Search: Here‘s What Drives Visibility](https://www.semrush.com/blog/linkedin-ai-visibility-study/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Semrush’s **17-month, 1B+ row ChatGPT clickstream analysis** is also worth retaining because it changes how we model AI visibility. ChatGPT outbound referrals grew **206% year over year in 2025**, but as of February 2026 its web-search feature was used on only about **34.5% of observed queries**, and that percentage had ranged from roughly 15% to 66% during the study. More than **20% of outbound ChatGPT referrals went to Google**. The reusable rule is therefore: do not model ChatGPT visibility as “web retrieval SEO only.” Separate **retrieval-dependent visibility**, **pre-existing brand/entity association**, and the subsequent **handoff into Google/branded search** when measuring the buyer journey. [ChatGPT traffic analysis: Insights from 17 months of clickstream data](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-search-insights/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

One additional reporting workflow from Ahrefs is worth keeping: judge SEO against the **market**, not only against your own traffic curve. Build a representative keyword/topic cluster, monitor its aggregate demand over time, and track your competitive share alongside absolute traffic. Flat traffic while the category is shrinking can represent share gains; growing traffic while category demand grows faster can hide competitive loss. Ahrefs explicitly treats Share of Voice, Traffic Value and related measures as **proxies/leading indicators rather than causal revenue proof**, which is the right evidence discipline for executive SEO reporting. [MBA Metrics for SEO: How to Speak Leadership's Language](https://ahrefs.com/blog/mba-metrics-for-seo/)
