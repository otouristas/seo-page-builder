### Newly added — **Google requirements / official guidance**

- **Preferred Sources is becoming an actionable publisher tactic, not just a user-side feature.** As of **August 20, 2026**, Google documents a custom interactive button publishers can place on-site so users can designate the site as a preferred source and return to the original page afterward. Preferred Sources had already expanded into **AI Overviews and AI Mode** in May. New workflow rule: for eligible publisher/content sites, evaluate a Preferred Sources CTA as part of retention/distribution strategy; do **not** treat it as a ranking-factor hack. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

- **Migration SOP update:** when changing domains, account for **all domain/subdomain variants**, including `www` and non-`www`, and use Change of Address appropriately for those variants. Migration QA should explicitly inventory these variants rather than treating only the canonical hostname as the migration target. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

- **Review-schema/reputation rule:** Google added explicit guidance against **fake or undisclosed incentivized reviews** on July 24, 2026. Review-generation workflows therefore need disclosure/compliance checks, not merely schema validity. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

- **Product schema edge case:** for sales, Google now documents `validFrom`, `validThrough`, and `priceValidUntil` for defining sale-price windows, including placement on `Offer` or `PriceSpecification`. Ecommerce schema audits should validate temporal sale data rather than only `price`/`availability`. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

- **Image-selection signal:** Google explicitly says both **schema.org image markup and `og:image`** may be used when selecting thumbnails for Search and Discover. Operationally, these should point to the same preferred high-quality image unless there is a deliberate reason not to. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

- **Googlebot raw-HTML ceiling is an audit condition:** Google clarified the default processing limit in 2026; Ahrefs summarizes the practical implication as Googlebot processing only the first **2 MB of raw HTML**. For unusually bloated SSR pages, audits should verify that critical content, internal links and schema are not positioned beyond that boundary. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

### Newly added — **vendor research / observations, not Google rules**

- **AI Overviews are moving materially into commercial research queries.** Semrush studied 600K+ US desktop keywords across 10 industries from Nov 2025–Apr 2026: commercial-intent AIO presence increased **71%**, while transactional AIO presence declined **5%**. Travel was among the sectors with a particularly large commercial-vs-transactional gap. Practical workflow: AI-visibility tracking should no longer focus only on informational keywords—include comparison, “best”, alternatives, category-selection and pre-purchase research queries. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/)

- That research also found **AIOs and Google Ads increasingly coexist**, and many higher-CPC research queries trigger AIOs. This supports an integrated SERP-opportunity model: when prioritizing commercial keywords, map **organic ranking + AIO citation + paid presence** together instead of treating SEO/PPC/AEO as independent channels. This is an observed market pattern, **not evidence that CPC causes AIO inclusion**. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/)

- **Do not turn “AI-written vs human-written” into a Google rule.** Semrush analyzed 20K keywords / 42K blog posts and found pages classified as human-written substantially overrepresented at position #1; purely AI-classified content reached #1 only 9% of the time in their dataset. The durable inference is narrower: retain human editorial judgment, proprietary knowledge, SME input and originality. The study does **not** demonstrate that Google penalizes AI authorship itself. [Does AI content rank well in search? Survey + Data study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/does-ai-content-rank-in-search-data-study/)

- **Useful cannibalization diagnostic:** Ahrefs documented a case where publishing multiple similarly targeted “SEO case study” pages caused ranking replacement/swapping. Creating a central pillar page linking to the individual cases produced a stable top-three result. Added workflow rule: when ranking history shows repeated URL swapping among same-intent pages, test an **intent-level hub/consolidation architecture** rather than blindly rewriting every competing URL. This is a case study, not a universal ranking rule. [15 Fast & Proven Ways to Increase Organic Traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-increase-organic-traffic/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The strongest addition from this pass is the **commercial-AIO shift**: GEO/AEO keyword sets should now explicitly cover the *mid-funnel research layer*, especially comparisons and selection queries in travel, finance, technology and other considered-purchase verticals. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/)
