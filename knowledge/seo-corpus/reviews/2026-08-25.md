### New durable additions to the SEO Skill Library

**Google requirements / official guidance**

- **Crawl-budget work should be conditional, not a default audit task.** Google now frames crawl-budget optimization mainly for ~1M+ page sites with moderate change, ~10K+ page sites changing daily, or sites with substantial `Discovered – currently not indexed` inventory. For ordinary sites where new pages are crawled promptly, sitemap hygiene + Page Indexing monitoring is sufficient. [Crawl Budget Management | Google Crawling Infrastructure  |  Crawling infrastructure  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **For large sites, `noindex` is not a crawl-budget control.** Googlebot must still fetch the URL to discover the directive. If URLs genuinely should never be crawled—faceted/sorted duplicates, infinite-scroll duplicates, etc.—robots.txt can reduce wasted crawling. But Google explicitly warns that blocking URLs does **not automatically redistribute** that capacity to important URLs unless the hostname is already hitting its crawl-capacity limit. [Crawl Budget Management | Google Crawling Infrastructure  |  Crawling infrastructure  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **304 responses are a technical SEO efficiency lever at scale.** Supporting `If-Modified-Since`/`ETag` → `304 Not Modified` lets Google reuse cached content and reduces server resource consumption. This belongs in large-site crawl-efficiency audits alongside inventory control and server capacity. [Crawl Budget Management | Google Crawling Infrastructure  |  Crawling infrastructure  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Crawl budget is hostname-specific.** `www.example.com` and `code.example.com` are treated as separate crawling sites, with separate crawl budgets. This is useful when diagnosing subdomain migrations, large marketplaces and multi-platform stacks. [Crawl Budget Management | Google Crawling Infrastructure  |  Crawling infrastructure  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Google clarified another canonicalization edge case: **same-language regional URLs may be clustered as duplicates**, so regional versions such as US/UK should combine canonicalization signals with `hreflang`. Conversely, URLs where only navigation/footer is translated but the primary content remains in the original language can still be considered duplicates. [What is URL Canonicalization | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization?hl=en&utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Canonical selection incorporates multiple signals—redirects, sitemap inclusion, HTTPS and `rel=canonical` among them—but the declared canonical remains a **hint rather than a command**. Google also states that the canonical is generally the URL crawled most regularly, while duplicates are crawled less often. [What is URL Canonicalization | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization?hl=en&utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Preferred Sources now extends into AI Mode and AI Overviews**, and as of August 20 Google documents a custom interactive button sites can use to let readers select them as a preferred source. This creates a new owned-audience / AI-search optimization tactic, but it should not be confused with a normal ranking signal. [Latest Google Search Documentation Updates | Google Search Central  |  What's new  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/updates?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Vendor observations / studies — not Google ranking requirements**

- Ahrefs' useful formulation for content differentiation is now: **SERP coverage first, information gain second.** Cover the concepts required to satisfy core intent, then deliberately identify what every ranking result omits and add proprietary data, first-hand experience, novel examples, expert evidence or an underserved angle. Merely paraphrasing competitors adds essentially no semantic novelty. Crucially, Ahrefs acknowledges that Google's patented “information gain score” is **not confirmed as a live ranking system**. [What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- This suggests a stronger refresh workflow than standard content-gap analysis: build a **SERP overlap matrix → identify consensus information → identify missing/underserved intent → add defensible proprietary evidence**. Forum/Reddit questions ranking on page one can be treated as an intent-discovery signal when editorial results fail to answer the question adequately—not as evidence that “Reddit rankings” themselves are a ranking factor. [What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- For emerging-keyword discovery, Ahrefs adds a useful validation workflow: don't trust a high growth % alone. Check the **12-month shape**, first-seen date and actual SERP strength. A staircase-like trend is more defensible than a single spike, and low KD can be misleading when category leaders dominate the SERP. Growth metrics should therefore be treated as directional rather than forecasts. [15 Fast & Proven Ways to Increase Organic Traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-increase-organic-traffic/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Semrush's 600K-keyword study found AI Overviews expanding beyond informational queries: commercial-intent AIO occurrence increased **71%** over its Nov 2025–Apr 2026 US desktop sample, while transactional AIO occurrence fell 5%. This is strong evidence against treating GEO/AIO optimization as purely top-of-funnel informational SEO; commercial comparison/category content should also be monitored. This remains observational data, not a Google rule. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- A particularly useful AI-visibility edge case: Semrush's GPT-5.2 experiment found only **25.6% overlap in cited domains** between minimal- and high-reasoning responses for identical prompts. Higher reasoning produced more fan-out searches and more citations, while government, academic and official documentation gained citation share and UGC lost share. Therefore AI visibility testing should **segment by reasoning/retrieval behavior rather than treating “ChatGPT visibility” as one static SERP**. [Only 25% of cited sources overlap between ChatGPT‘s different reasoning modes Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-reasoning-ai-visibility/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- That experiment also supports a new AEO testing principle: evaluate brands across an entire **problem → exploration → comparison → validation → selection journey**, not just isolated prompts. Semrush observed some brands persisting across funnel stages under higher reasoning, implying that authoritative TOFU/reference material can influence later-stage inclusion. The sample was only 100 prompts/20 journeys, so this belongs in the corpus as an experimental hypothesis rather than a universal rule. [Only 25% of cited sources overlap between ChatGPT‘s different reasoning modes Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-reasoning-ai-visibility/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The most consequential additions today are the **crawl-budget decision thresholds**, the distinction between **SERP coverage vs. true information gain**, and the requirement to treat **AI visibility as model/reasoning-mode dependent rather than one universal ranking environment**.
