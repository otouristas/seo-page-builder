### Google requirements / official guidance

- **Robots meta tags outside `<head>` are still respected by Google.** Google recommends putting robots directives in `<head>`, but explicitly documents that it also honors them in the `<body>`. This is useful for debugging malformed templates and JS/CMS output: a stray `noindex` injected into body content can still suppress a URL. Also, when directives conflict, Google applies the **more restrictive rule**. [Robots Meta Tags Specifications | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag)
- **`nosnippet` now has a direct generative-search consequence.** Google states that `nosnippet` prevents content from being used as a **direct input for AI Overviews and AI Mode**, in addition to suppressing traditional snippets. This belongs in AEO/GEO audits because snippet-control directives are now also AI-visibility controls. [Robots Meta Tags Specifications | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag)
- **Google has documented requirements for “Read more” deep links.** To improve the chance of section-level deep links appearing in snippets, target content should already be visible rather than hidden in tabs/accordions; page-load JavaScript should not forcibly reset scroll position; and History API/hash manipulation must preserve the URL fragment. This is an actionable technical/content QA rule for long guides and TOC-driven pages. [How to Write Meta Descriptions | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)
- **Undisclosed incentivized reviews can invalidate review-rich-result eligibility.** Google now explicitly prohibits fake reviews and reviews exchanged for money, discounts, vouchers, free products, etc. **unless the incentive is clearly and prominently disclosed**. This should be checked alongside review schema validation, especially for ecommerce, affiliate, hospitality, SaaS and review-heavy sites. [Review Snippet (Review, AggregateRating) Structured Data | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)

### Ahrefs research / observations — not Google rules

Ahrefs analyzed **145M US SERPs** and found Google’s Discussions & Forums feature on about **11.7%** of them. When the feature appears, Reddit dominates, but the more durable finding is query structure: **98.7%** of appearances were associated with informational intent and the feature becomes substantially more common as queries get longer; **43.2% involved 5+ word queries**. Ahrefs also found **no correlation between backlinks to individual discussion threads and how frequently those threads appeared**. [Analysis: Reddit Appears in 83.9% of Google's Discussions and Forums Results](https://ahrefs.com/blog/discussions-and-forums-serps/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Reusable workflow:** when researching long-tail informational SERPs, explicitly check whether Google is allocating SERP real estate to discussions/forums. If yes, evaluate whether the intent contains an experience/opinion/community component rather than assuming another conventional article is the right asset. Do **not** infer that simply launching a new forum or acquiring thread-level backlinks will reproduce Reddit-style visibility—the dataset does not support that conclusion. [Analysis: Reddit Appears in 83.9% of Google's Discussions and Forums Results](https://ahrefs.com/blog/discussions-and-forums-serps/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Ahrefs’ information-gain review also surfaces a useful distinction that belongs in the evidence framework: Google has a patent describing **session/user-relative information gain and semantic duplicate-fact suppression**, including conversational-agent use cases, but Ahrefs correctly notes that this does **not prove the patented system is a live ranking factor**. Therefore, “information gain” should remain a content-quality/workflow heuristic, not something we report as a confirmed ranking signal. [What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Content-production workflow worth retaining

Ahrefs’ current AI editorial process offers a durable operational pattern rather than another “AI content ranks” claim: separate AI-assisted production into **idea → outline → evidence → draft gates**, with human approval between stages. The particularly useful rule is to front-load proprietary inputs—interviews, internal knowledge, original data, demonstrations, failed experiments and real examples—before generation instead of asking a model to manufacture differentiation from the same public SERP corpus. [How We Use AI for Every Article Without Making AI Slop](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-we-use-ai-without-making-ai-slop/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That reinforces a stronger reusable QA principle for the library:

**AI efficiency should be reinvested into evidence and information gain, not simply page volume.** A content pipeline should be able to reject an article before drafting if it has no defensible contribution beyond the current SERP. [How We Use AI for Every Article Without Making AI Slop](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-we-use-ai-without-making-ai-slop/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
