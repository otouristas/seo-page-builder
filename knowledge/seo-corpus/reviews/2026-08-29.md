### Google requirements / official guidance

- **Googlebot’s normal fetch limit is now a hard technical-audit check:** Googlebot crawls only the first **2 MB of supported non-PDF files**, measured on the **uncompressed** response. PDFs have a separate **64 MB** limit. CSS/JS resources are fetched independently and subject to their respective limits. Critical indexable HTML, canonical/meta directives, internal links, structured data, and primary content therefore should not depend on markup appearing beyond the cutoff. [What Is Googlebot | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/googlebot?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Discover expertise is evaluated topic-by-topic, not simply site-wide.** Google’s February 2026 Discover update explicitly says a broad site can establish expertise in an individual topic/section. This supports building strong topical sections inside authoritative multi-topic domains rather than assuming Discover requires a single-topic site. Google simultaneously increased emphasis on **original, in-depth, timely, locally relevant content** and reduced sensational/clickbait material. [Google's February 2026 Discover Core Update  |  Google Search Central Blog  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Discover/image QA should explicitly validate the preferred image.** Google can use both `og:image` and schema.org metadata to determine thumbnails. Discover recommends ≥1200 px width, >300K pixels, landscape-friendly imagery, `max-image-preview:large`, and avoiding logos/generic or text-heavy images. This belongs in publishing QA rather than treating OG images as only social metadata. [Get on Discover | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Vendor research / experiments — not Google ranking rules

- **Do not sell schema as a direct GEO/AEO citation booster.** Ahrefs tracked **1,885 pages that added JSON-LD** against ~4,000 matched controls and used difference-in-differences analysis. Citation changes were approximately **+2.4% AI Mode** and **+2.2% ChatGPT**, both statistically indistinguishable from zero; AIO citations declined 4.6%, but Ahrefs could not confidently attribute that decline to schema. Schema remains useful for eligible rich results, entity clarity and machine-readable data—but “add schema → gain AI citations” is currently unsupported. [We Tracked 1,885 Pages Adding Schema. AI Citations Barely Moved.](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)
  **Reusable experiment rule:** whenever testing GEO changes, use matched control URLs and remove platform-wide growth/decline from the analysis. Raw before/after AI-visibility comparisons are particularly misleading because the platforms themselves change rapidly. [We Tracked 1,885 Pages Adding Schema. AI Citations Barely Moved.](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)

- **ChatGPT visibility should be measured by reasoning complexity and buyer-journey stage, not as one aggregate metric.** Semrush’s GPT-5.2 experiment found only **25.6% overlap in cited domains** between minimal- and high-reasoning responses to the same prompts. High reasoning produced more citations and substantially more fan-out research; the largest retrieval intensity occurred around **comparison and selection** questions. Official docs, reference material, government/academic sources gained citation share while Reddit/UGC declined under deeper reasoning. Treat this as directional vendor research, not a universal ChatGPT rule. [Only 25% of cited sources overlap between ChatGPT‘s different reasoning modes Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-reasoning-ai-visibility/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

One additional evidence-quality rule is worth retaining: **do not interpret “AI-generated content” itself as a ranking penalty.** Ahrefs’ July 2026 analysis of 331K pages found fully/highly AI-classified pages throughout the top 10 and no evidence of a delayed blanket suppression pattern. The stronger association was between lower-quality/heavily automated output and weaker performance. Ahrefs also explicitly warns that AI detectors are probabilistic. This matters because Semrush’s separate detector-based study reported much worse outcomes for “pure AI” content; the disagreement reinforces that **detector classification ≠ causal ranking factor**. [Google Doesn’t Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (331k Pages Studied)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/)
