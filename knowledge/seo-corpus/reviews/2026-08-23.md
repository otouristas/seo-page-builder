### **Google Search Central — requirements / direct guidance**

**Canonical troubleshooting is now more explicitly content-led, not just tag-led.** Google’s updated canonicalization documentation says that when two URLs remain clustered despite technically correct canonicals, the practical fix is often to make the pages **sufficiently and meaningfully different**. After changes, Google may keep them in the same duplicate cluster for **up to roughly two weeks**, with larger content differences generally separating faster. Request Indexing should be reserved for priority URLs because of quotas. [Fix Canonicalization Issues | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)

**New audit rule:** when investigating `Duplicate, Google chose different canonical`, do not stop at canonicals/sitemaps/redirects. Audit whether the URLs actually contain enough differentiated primary content to justify independent indexing.

Google also explicitly highlights two useful edge cases:

- CMS/plugin errors can inject unintended canonicals or redirects, so inspect the **rendered HTML/server response**, not merely CMS settings.
- Cross-domain canonical anomalies can result from server misconfiguration or hacking, including unrelated hosts returning identical soft-404 content. [Fix Canonicalization Issues | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)

And for syndicated content, Google says `rel=canonical` is **not its recommended anti-duplication mechanism**; if preventing the partner copy from appearing matters, having syndication partners block indexing is more reliable. [Fix Canonicalization Issues | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)

### **Ahrefs — observations / experiments, not Google rules**

A 2026 Ahrefs study covering roughly **331K analyzable ranking/indexed pages** reinforces a distinction worth baking into our AI-content audits: **AI percentage itself should not be treated as a penalty trigger.** Fully or predominantly AI-created pages occur even in positions 1–3, and Ahrefs found no obvious delayed “AI cliff” where highly AI-generated content systematically collapses after several months. [Google Doesn’t Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (331k Pages Studied)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/)

However, performance declined directionally as estimated AI use increased: low/moderate-AI pages received roughly **2–3× more impressions** than high/very-high-AI pages, while indexation dropped from ~49% in the lowest-AI bucket to ~40% in the highest. Ahrefs explicitly warns this is **correlation**, with content quality, authority and site characteristics as confounders. [Google Doesn’t Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (331k Pages Studied)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/)

**Reusable rule:** never recommend “humanizing text to beat AI detection.” Instead QA the failure modes that frequently accompany scaled AI: commodity information, factual errors, lack of first-hand evidence, weak linking, no visuals, and no original contribution.

A separate Ahrefs AI-visibility experiment produced a useful warning about **self-serving “best X” pages**. They can cause AI systems to discover a brand, but citation ≠ recommendation: in one experiment, an AI cited Ahrefs' event-comparison page yet recommended competitors listed on that same page **43% of the time**. Citations were also highly volatile over time. [Self-Promotional Content Works—Until It Backfires (AI SEO Experiment)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/)

**AEO workflow update:** track separately:

`PAGE CITED → BRAND MENTIONED → BRAND RECOMMENDED`

Do not report citation count alone as AI-search success.

### **Information gain — useful framework, but do not misrepresent it as a Google ranking factor**

Ahrefs' August 20 analysis makes an important evidentiary distinction: Google owns an information-gain patent and Google's public quality guidance repeatedly emphasizes originality/additional value, but **Google has never confirmed that a literal “information gain score” is deployed as a ranking factor**. [What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)

The durable content workflow is nevertheless excellent:

**SERP consensus → required coverage → unanswered/underserved intent → proprietary evidence.**

Simply paraphrasing competing pages does not create information gain because semantic systems can recognize equivalent meaning. The defensible additions are things such as original datasets, firsthand experiments, expert observations, proprietary examples, actual screenshots/results, and questions appearing in forums/PAA that ranking pages fail to answer. [What Is Information Gain in SEO? (and How To Add It to Your Content)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)

That fits neatly with Google's newer **“valuable, non-commodity content”** guidance already in the library.

### **Semrush — AI-search studies / observational evidence**

One finding materially changes AI visibility measurement: Semrush tested identical buyer-journey prompts under different ChatGPT reasoning settings and found only **25.6% overlap in cited domains**. Higher reasoning increased citation frequency from 50% to 68%, average cited sources from 2.6 to 4.5, and produced **4.6× more fan-out searches**. Official/government/academic sources gained share while UGC lost share under higher reasoning. [Only 25% of cited sources overlap between ChatGPT‘s different reasoning modes Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-reasoning-ai-visibility/)

**New measurement rule:** never treat “ChatGPT visibility” as one homogeneous SERP. Benchmark at minimum:

`platform/model × reasoning mode × prompt class × buyer-journey stage`

For complex comparison/research queries, content must be eligible across the **fan-out subqueries**, not merely optimized for the user's literal prompt.

Semrush's commercial-intent AIO study also deserves inclusion. Across 600K+ U.S. desktop keywords, commercial-intent SERPs triggering AI Overviews increased **71% in six months**, while transactional AIO prevalence declined ~5%. Travel was among the categories showing a particularly large commercial-vs-transactional gap. [AI Overviews are expanding across commercial intent search Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/)

**Practical consequence for travel SEO:** AEO/GEO opportunity is increasingly concentrated in the **research/comparison layer**—“best area,” “car vs ATV,” “hotel A vs B,” “where to stay,” “is X worth it,” itinerary choices, pros/cons—rather than only the final booking query.

Finally, Semrush's 1,000-domain backlink study found AI visibility correlated more strongly with **authority/quality and referring-domain diversity** than raw backlink volume, with evidence of threshold effects rather than linear gains. Nofollow and image links also correlated with AI visibility in their dataset. This remains correlation, **not evidence that AI systems directly use these link attributes as ranking factors**. [Do Backlinks Still Matter in AI Search? Insights from 1,000 Domains Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/backlinks-ai-search-study/)

### Highest-value additions to the SEO framework

The three strongest changes are:

**1. Canonical problems can be content-differentiation problems.** Add content uniqueness analysis to canonical debugging.

**2. AI visibility needs a three-stage KPI:** citation → brand mention → recommendation.

**3. Optimize AI discovery across fan-out journeys, not one prompt.** Reasoning depth substantially changes which sources get retrieved.

I’ve treated Google's statements as authoritative guidance and kept all Ahrefs/Semrush findings explicitly classified as experiments, correlations, or vendor observations rather than Google ranking requirements.
