### Google requirements / official guidance

- **Sitemaps can be used as a canonicalization signal, but only weakly compared with redirects and `rel="canonical"`.** Google explicitly recommends listing the URLs you actually want indexed and generally expects sitemap URLs to be canonical URLs. This makes sitemap/canonical disagreement a concrete technical QA issue, not just housekeeping. [Build and Submit a Sitemap | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Sitemap scope depends on where the sitemap is hosted unless submitted through Search Console.** A sitemap discovered normally affects URLs below its parent directory; a root-level sitemap can cover the whole site. This becomes an edge case for large sites using section-level sitemap files, unusual CMS paths, CDN-hosted assets, or dynamically generated sitemap endpoints. [Build and Submit a Sitemap | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **`lastmod` should represent a meaningful page change, not build/deploy time.** Google uses sufficiently reliable `lastmod` data as a crawl-scheduling signal. Therefore, regenerating today's timestamp for every URL on every deployment is counterproductive because it destroys the signal's reliability. The reusable rule is: update `lastmod` only when primary content or other materially search-relevant page data changes. [Sitemaps ping endpoint is going away  |  Google Search Central Blog  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/06/sitemaps-lastmod-ping?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **International SEO edge case:** Google treats several nominal country-code domains—including `.ai`, `.io`, `.co`, `.me`, `.tv`, and others—as **generic TLDs rather than strong geographic targeting signals**. This matters when auditing domain architecture or recommending international domains: do not assume a ccTLD-looking extension automatically geotargets that country. [Managing Multi-Regional and Multilingual Sites | Google Search Central  |  Documentation  |  Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/crawling/managing-multi-regional-sites?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Semrush research — correlation/observational evidence, not Google rules

A strong new distinction emerged from Semrush's 2026 ChatGPT topical-authority study:

**Citation authority and brand-recognition authority behave differently.**

Across **1,094 categories, 283,215 domain-category citation observations, 76,493 brand mentions, and 1,458 mapped brands**, domains could continue earning citations well outside their core topics, but **named brand mentions depended much more strongly on topical proximity and depth**. In closely related categories, brands were cited in 74% of appearances and named in 44%; in distant categories those fell to 50% and 25%. More importantly, citation + named-brand appearances were **34% in close categories versus only 9% in distant ones**. [How topical authority spreads (and where it doesn’t) in ChatGPT Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topical-focus-study/)

This produces an important reusable GEO strategy rule:

> **Do not measure topical authority only by citations.** Track separately:
> **source citation share → brand mention share → cited + mentioned share.**

A site may become a useful retrieval source without the model actually associating its **brand** with the category. [How topical authority spreads (and where it doesn’t) in ChatGPT Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topical-focus-study/)

The same study also found that **depth beat breadth for brand recognition**. A brand appearing in only one of five prompts inside many categories did not build strong mention share; the negative pattern disappeared only around **3/5 prompt coverage within a category**. This is not a magic threshold or ranking factor, but it supports a practical expansion workflow: establish repeat visibility across a core topic before expanding into semantically adjacent topics. [How topical authority spreads (and where it doesn’t) in ChatGPT Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topical-focus-study/)

### Ahrefs experiment — useful GEO warning

Ahrefs' self-promotion experiment adds a particularly important distinction between **being retrieved, being cited, and being recommended**.

Across **34 promotional pages and 9,886 AI responses** from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot, AI assistants sometimes cited a company's own roundup yet recommended its competitors. For the conference experiment, **43% of responses citing the promotional page still did not recommend the promoted conference**. When the page was merely retrieved but not cited, the failure rate rose to **74%**. [Self-Promotional Content Works—Until It Backfires (AI SEO Experiment)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This adds two reusable GEO measurement rules:

**Citation ≠ recommendation ≠ brand mention.** These should be separate KPIs.

And AI visibility needs **longitudinal measurement**, not screenshots. Ahrefs found roughly a quarter of page/query/engine citations appeared once and never returned, while recurring citations appeared on only about **one-third of eligible days** between their first and last appearance. [Self-Promotional Content Works—Until It Backfires (AI SEO Experiment)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/)

The experiment also supports a cautious tactical hypothesis: self-authored comparisons appear most useful where there is an **awareness gap + strong category fit + credible brand trust**. Ahrefs Evolve appeared in **66.4%** of “best SEO conferences” responses but only **15.8%** for the much broader “best marketing conferences” query. Treat this as experimental evidence, not a universal optimization rule. [Self-Promotional Content Works—Until It Backfires (AI SEO Experiment)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/)
